## 3. Why young women don't seek care

| Finding | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Shame is the central barrier to young people's use of sexual health services across Asia-Pacific (31 studies, 1,800+ young people); clinics are a last resort | [Systematic review, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11812895/) |
| 44.4% of Mongolian women who chose HPV self-sampling did so to avoid embarrassment at the gynecological exam | [APJCP 2022](https://journal.waocp.org/article_90402.html) |
| 95.1% of young Mongolian women found self-sampling easy; all who chose it cited privacy and reduced anxiety | [APJCP 2024](https://journal.waocp.org/article_91149.html) |
| In Ulaanbaatar family health centers, 2–3 doctors often share one exam room; STI and sexual health services are under-provided | [MoH / UNFPA / UNICEF PHC assessment, 2024](https://mongolia.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/pub-pdf/2025-03/PHC_b5_nom%202_eng.pdf) |
| A young mother said confidentiality mattered most to her; she hadn't known a free adolescent health room existed | [UNFPA Mandukhai pilot](https://asiapacific.unfpa.org/en/mandukhai-pilot-project) |
| Students say school health classes don't really cover sex | [Global Press Journal](https://globalpressjournal.com/asia/mongolia/silence-around-sex-dangerous-consequences-mongolian-teens/) |

## 4. What this means for the design

1. The services exist and are often free. The gap is demand: information, trust and privacy.
2. Shame and fear of being seen are the main barriers, so anonymity is a core feature, not an extra.
3. Misinformation spreads on social media, so doctor-reviewed answers must reach people in the same channels.
4. Chlamydia and latent syphilis are silent, so prompting a test after risk matters more than waiting for symptoms.
